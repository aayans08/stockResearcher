# Thesis: AI Infrastructure

> **Root claim:** Frontier-model training and inference scale faster than every physical input they depend on, so value migrates down the stack from chips (now consensus) to whatever becomes scarce next. As of July 2026 the four hyperscalers alone have guided ~$725B of 2026 capex (~77% above 2025), and the binding constraints have visibly shifted from GPU supply to **electricity delivery** (interconnection queues of 4–7 years, transformer lead times of 4–5 years, gas-turbine slots sold out through ~2028), **heat rejection** (liquid cooling as design default above ~60 kW racks), **optical bandwidth** (1.6T module demand up ~17x y/y; Nvidia paying $4B to secure optics supply), and **materials** (GOES, copper, ABF film). The frontier is no longer "who builds data centers" — it is the second- and third-order supply chains those data centers exhaust. Several picks-and-shovels trades (gas turbines, Bloom, HBM) have already re-rated hard and are treated as crowded below.

## [CONSENSUS] Root: AI boom — scaling of frontier training + inference

**Claim:** Frontier labs and hyperscalers are converting record capital into compute faster than the physical economy can supply power, packaging, cooling, and bandwidth, making each successive input scarce in turn.

**Evidence:**
- Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Microsoft have guided roughly **$725B of combined 2026 capex** (Amazon ~$200B, Google $175–185B, Meta $115–135B, Microsoft $110–120B), up ~77% from ~$410B in 2025, ~75% AI-related ([Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/ai-spending), 2026; [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/hyperscalers-hit-700-billion-2026-111243744.html), 2026).
- Nvidia's April quarter (Q1 FY2027, reported 2026-05-20) showed data-center revenue nearly doubling y/y, following record Q4 FY2026 data-center revenue of ~$62B, with Blackwell effectively sold out ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/20/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q1-2027.html), 2026-05-20; [NVIDIA Newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-2026), 2026-02).
- Demand is broadening beyond the big four: Anthropic announced a **$50B** US data-center program with Fluidstack; SoftBank announced a 5 GW, up-to-€75B AI campus in northern France ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-invests-50-billion-in-american-ai-infrastructure), 2025-11; [Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-construction/new-data-center-developments-july-2026), 2026-07).
- Markets are starting to price the capex-to-revenue gap, i.e., the boom is fully in consensus view ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jasonkirsch/2026/06/02/the-ai-capex-to-revenue-gap-is-widening---and-markets-are-starting-to-notice/), 2026-06-02).

**Exposed:** `NVDA` (compute anchor), `MSFT`/`AMZN`/`GOOGL`/`META` (capex sources, not derivatives), `TSM` (fab chokepoint). These are context, not the research edge.

**Status rationale:** CONSENSUS — the boom itself is front-page macro; the exercise is finding what it breaks next.

**Update (2026-09-20) — a new legal/political risk vector: a federal antitrust suit over the "pacing" coordination itself.** Following Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's 2026-09-12 essay "We Must Pace the Frontier" (call for frontier labs to slow capability-scaling velocity via outside evaluators and cross-lab safety coordination) and public backing from OpenAI's Altman, xAI's Musk and Google DeepMind's Hassabis, a federal antitrust lawsuit was filed 2026-09-18 in the N.D. Against this, Nvidia's Jensen Huang reaffirmed aggressive demand at a mid-September Scotland event: chip unit sales expected to roughly double in 2027 vs. 2026, with supply/capacity commitments raised from $119B to $279B — i.e., the chip-supply side of the chain is still signaling acceleration even as the equity market and now the courts react to the pacing rhetoric ([247wallst.com](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/17/nvidia-expects-chip-sales-to-double-in-2027/), 2026-09-17). No status change to the root — this sharpens a genuinely new political/legal risk without changing the underlying capex trajectory; see Neoclouds and Data-center REIT nodes below for the "Dies if" language this now warrants.

###### [SPECULATIVE] Mature-node DRAM/NAND crowd-out

**Claim:** AI-driven HBM/server-DRAM allocation is consuming so much of the top memory makers' advanced-process capacity that mainstream consumer/PC-grade DRAM and NAND are becoming scarce as a side effect, pricing power that could flow to legacy/mature-node memory capacity holders who aren't competing for HBM capex.

**Evidence:**
- Over 80% of advanced-process capacity at the top-3 memory makers (Samsung, SK hynix, Micron) is now reportedly allocated to AI-server memory, crowding out consumer/PC-grade DRAM and NAND; PC DRAM contract prices rose 13–18% QoQ and NAND 10–15% QoQ in Q3 2026 (moderating from 60% QoQ spikes in Q2) ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/ram/memory-price-surge-begins-to-cool-as-consumers-hit-affordability-limit-ai-demand-still-keeps-dram-and-nand-prices-climbing-through-q3-2026), 2026).
- 2026 supply-demand gaps for DRAM/NAND/HBM are described as the worst since 2011 (4.9%/4.2%/5.1% respectively) ([TechInsights](https://www.techinsights.com/blog/why-ai-memory-shortage-could-keep-dram-and-nand-prices-high-years), 2026).

**Exposed:** Nanya Technology (2408.TW, Taiwan), Winbond Electronics (2344.TW, Taiwan) — legacy/mature-node DRAM and NAND makers with spare non-AI capacity, positioned to capture pricing power without competing for HBM capex. **Evidence thin** on this specific pairing: the market-structure evidence (capacity-allocation and price-gap data) is solid, but no dated, company-specific earnings or pricing disclosure from Nanya or Winbond confirming they are capturing this pricing power was found this week — this is reasoned inference from the second-order chain, not yet a company-level data point.

**Dies if:** the top-3 memory makers redirect meaningful advanced-process capacity back toward consumer DRAM/NAND (easing the crowd-out) before Nanya/Winbond or comparable mature-node makers show a dated, disclosed pricing/margin benefit, or a demand-side affordability ceiling (already visible in the Q2→Q3 2026 price-growth deceleration) caps consumer DRAM/NAND pricing before any mature-node beneficiary captures it.

**Status rationale:** SPECULATIVE, new node (2026-09-20) — a genuine second-order chokepoint (AI capacity allocation squeezing a downstream, non-AI market) with solid macro sourcing, but the named beneficiaries are inferred rather than confirmed this week; watch for a dated Nanya/Winbond pricing or earnings disclosure before considering promotion.

**Update (2026-08-30) — even the apex of the AI stack is now visibly memory-constrained.** Nvidia's Q2 FY2027 print (2026-08-26/27) beat on revenue ($96.2B, data-center revenue $89B, +117% y/y) but guided gross margin down to 71–72% by fiscal Q4 (from 75% currently), citing rising memory costs explicitly; shares fell ~1.6–1.8% despite the beat ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-nvda-tops-q2-estimates-183248574.html), 2026-08-27; [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/nvidia-q2-fy27-slides-revenue-doubles-to-96b-data-center-surges-93CH-4878041), 2026-08-27). No status change — the trade is still fully CONSENSUS-priced — but this is the sharpest evidence yet that the *physical* scarcity underlying the node is real and intensifying, not just a memory-industry earnings story.

### [CURRENT] Neoclouds — rented GPU compute capacity

**Evidence:**
