# Thesis: GLP-1 Second-Order Effects

> **Root claim:** GLP-1 mass adoption is consensus and the first-order trade (Novo/Lilly) is largely played out on the long side — so the alpha has migrated to the second order: the picks-and-shovels GLP-1 makes *scarce* (qualified fill-finish and injector capacity, peptide API purification), the categories it makes *cheap or obsolete* (bariatric surgery, sleep-apnea implants, some dialysis, snacks/alcohol), and the *new necessities* it creates (muscle preservation, high-protein nutrition, oral/next-gen molecules). **Where the frontier is:** the market has correctly priced the drug makers and mostly priced the obvious device "losers" (Inspire, DaVita). The genuinely under-priced edges sit at (1) muscle-preservation / body-composition pharma — the next clinical must-have, still pre-consensus; (2) the *qualified-capacity* bottleneck in autoinjectors and coated stoppers, a physical constraint that survives even as drug prices fall; and (3) counter-narrative winners (ResMed, Stryker, protein/nutrition) where the reflexive "GLP-1 kills this" trade is wrong. Honesty flags throughout: the snack-apocalypse is overstated, and alcohol's decline is real in clinical data but hard to cleanly attribute in sales data.

## [CONSENSUS] GLP-1 mass adoption (the played-out first-order trade)

**Claim:** Incretin therapy has become a standard-of-care mega-category, so simply owning the two manufacturers is a crowded, well-understood trade rather than an edge.

**Evidence:**
- The global GLP-1 receptor-agonist market was ~$66.4B in 2025 and is projected at ~$82.0B in 2026, heading toward ~$185B by 2033 ([Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/glp-1-receptor-agonist-market), 2025).
- Lilly guided 2026 sales of $80–83B (~25% growth) while Novo is bracing for a *decline* in 2026 — the leadership has already flipped ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/04/eli-lilly-novo-nordisk-earnings-glp1-market.html), 2026-02-04).
- Lilly held >57% of US diabetes/obesity monthly scripts vs Novo's 43% by end-Q3 2025; Novo's first-mover lead has eroded ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/04/eli-lilly-novo-nordisk-earnings-glp1-market.html), 2026-02-04).

**Exposed:** `LLY` (winning share, oral + triple-agonist pipeline), `NVO` (share loser, margin pressure from price cuts), `VKTX` (Viking — oral/injectable follower, M&A candidate), `AMGN` (MariTide monthly dosing challenger).

**Status rationale:** CONSENSUS — every generalist owns LLY; the debate is now *which* incumbent, not *whether* the category is real. Root of the tree, not the edge.

### [CURRENT] Manufacturing & supply chain (picks-and-shovels)

**Claim:** Making a billion+ doses is engineering, not chemistry — the binding constraint is *qualified* fill-finish and injector capacity plus purification throughput, and that constraint persists even as list prices fall.

**Evidence:**
- Projected demand is ~1.5 billion devices annually for ~30M patients; GLP-1 demand is consuming high-value syringes, cartridges and coated stoppers faster than suppliers can validate new lines — "the constraint is qualified capacity, not raw materials" ([Pharma Bottlenecks](https://pharmabottlenecks.com/chokepoints/drug-packaging-components), 2025).
- Reverse-phase HPLC purification is the key bottleneck at commercial scale, driving accelerated CDMO capex on preparative HPLC ([DCAT Value Chain Insights](https://www.dcatvci.org/features/cdmo-cmo-expansion-update-riding-the-strength-of-tides/), 2025).
- North American peptide-manufacturing investment hit a five-year high in 2026 ([PeptideStaff](https://peptidestaff.com/news/cdmo-capacity-expansion-north-america-peptide-2026/), 2026).

**Exposed:** See grandchildren below. Net: this is where a physical scarcity survives the price war.

#### [CURRENT] Peptide CAPI / CDMO capacity

**Claim:** Semaglutide/cagrilintide are peptides that must be synthesized and purified at unprecedented scale, making contract peptide API and bioprocessing-consumable suppliers structurally short of capacity.

**Evidence:**
- Bachem is more than doubling Bubendorf capacity, with test batches from new lines starting Q2 2025 ([Bachem](https://www.bachem.com/knowledge-center/glp-1-demand-for-peptide-manufacturers/), 2025).
- PolyPeptide announced a €100M expansion to *double* SPPS capacity in Malmö, largely to serve one large commercial GLP-1 agreement ([DCAT](https://www.dcatvci.org/features/cdmo-cmo-expansion-update-riding-the-strength-of-tides/), 2025).
- The GLP-1 peptide-synthesis CDMO market is forecast to grow ~12.7% CAGR through 2034 ([InsightAce](https://www.insightaceanalytic.com/report/glp-1-peptide-synthesis-cdmo-market/3105), 2025).

**Exposed:** Bachem (BANB.SW / `BACHY`), PolyPeptide (PPGN.SW), Lonza (LONN.SW / `LZAGY`), `RGEN` (Repligen — chromatography/filtration consumables into the HPLC bottleneck), `DHR` (Danaher — bioprocessing tools), `AMPH` (Amphastar — peptide API/complex generics). Note oral small-molecule orforglipron *bypasses* peptide synthesis — a long-term risk to peptide-CDMO demand growth.

**Status rationale:** CURRENT — the capacity crunch is recognized by specialists but under-followed by generalists; Swiss/European listings keep it off most US screens. Orforglipron is the structural bear case.

#### [CURRENT] Fill-finish & injector devices (autoinjectors, pens, stoppers)

**Claim:** Every injectable dose needs a prefilled syringe/cartridge, a coated stopper and (increasingly) an autoinjector — a concentrated oligopoly whose qualified capacity is the true chokepoint.

**Evidence:**
- Stevanato/Gerresheimer dominate prefillable syringes and cartridges; SHL and Ypsomed own autoinjectors — SHL opened a $220M South Carolina plant targeting 1.5B units in 2025 ([Pharma Bottlenecks](https://pharmabottlenecks.com/chokepoints/drug-packaging-components), 2025).
- GLP-1/incretin therapies are now ~21–22% of Stevanato revenue ([Pharma Bottlenecks](https://pharmabottlenecks.com/chokepoints/drug-packaging-components), 2025).
- SCHOTT Pharma launched a 5.5mL large-volume prefillable syringe paired with Ypsomed's YpsoMate 5.5 autoinjector — capacity purpose-built for high-volume GLP-1 dosing ([drug-dev](https://drug-dev.com/special-feature-injection-devices-from-pens-autoinjectors-to-pills-sprays-capsules-injections-become-more-efficient/), 2025).
- West Pharmaceutical Q1 2026 revenue +21% y/y to $845M, adjusted EPS +47%, explicitly attributed to "accelerating demand in high-value injectable drug components... across biologics and GLP-1 therapies" — fundamentals, not just multiple expansion, are driving the re-rate ([Zacks/TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/zacks:48599b6dc094b:0-wst-stock-up-nearly-33-ytd-will-the-uptrend-continue-in-the-rest-of-2026/), 2026-07). West completed a $112.5M sale of SmartDose 3.5mL rights to AbbVie (2026-07-01) while retaining its 10mL SmartDose line — a strategic refocus toward larger-volume on-body delivery rather than a retreat ([Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/stocks/us/pharmaceuticals-biotech/nyse-wst/west-pharmaceutical-services/news/west-pharmaceutical-services-wst-sells-smartdose-rights-is-t), 2026-07-01). Morgan Stanley (to $365) and Evercore ISI (to $410) both raised WST price targets 2026-07-06.

**Exposed:** `STVN` (Stevanato), `WST` (West Pharmaceutical — coated stoppers/components, the most acute qualified-capacity node), Gerresheimer (GXI.DE), SCHOTT Pharma (1SXP.DE), Ypsomed (YPSN.SW), `ATR` (AptarGroup — drug delivery), `BDX` (Becton Dickinson — prefillable syringes). Risk: oral pills and quarterly/monthly dosing reduce per-patient device counts over time.

**Update (2026-07-19):** West's $112.5M sale of SmartDose 3.5mL rights to AbbVie (announced 2026-01-12) formally closed 2026-07-01, while it retains its 10mL SmartDose line — primary-source confirmation of the refocus toward larger-volume injectable/GLP-1 platforms already noted in-tree ([West Pharma IR](https://investor.westpharma.com/news-releases/news-release-details/west-signs-agreement-sell-manufacturing-and-supply-rights), 2026-01-12). WST reports Q2 2026 before market open **2026-07-23** ([West Pharma IR](https://investor.westpharma.com/news-releases/news-release-details/west-host-second-quarter-2026-conference-call), 2026-07-07) — the next real test of the "fundamentals not just re-rating" reasoning holding this node at CURRENT despite 3–4x SPY crowding.

**Dies if:** oral GLP-1 (orforglipron) or monthly/quarterly-dosing molecules capture more than ~30% of new GLP-1 starts, materially cutting devices-per-patient across the injectable base.

**Status rationale:** CURRENT — WST ran ~3.6x SPY trailing 6mo per this week's crowding check, but Q1 revenue growth (+21%, explicitly GLP-1-attributed) argues the move is substantially fundamentals-driven, not pure re-rating — reason to hold rather than demote. The "loser to pills" fear had de-rated some names, arguably too far given the installed injectable base; WST's Q2 print (due 2026-07-23) is the next real test.

### [CURRENT] Oral GLP-1s & next-generation molecules

**Claim:** Oral and higher-efficacy molecules (triple agonists, amylin combos) expand the addressable pool far beyond injectables and reset the efficacy bar to ~25–30% weight loss, reshuffling every downstream node.

**Evidence:**
- FDA approved Lilly's Foundayo (orforglipron), the first anytime-of-day oral GLP-1 pill for weight loss, on 2026-04-01; it is a non-peptide small molecule that sidesteps peptide-synthesis constraints ([PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fda-approves-lillys-foundayo-orforglipron-the-only-glp-1-pill-for-weight-loss-that-can-be-taken-any-time-of-day-without-food-or-water-restrictions-302731485.html), 2026-04-01).
- Retatrutide (GIP/GLP-1/glucagon triple agonist) delivered 28.3% mean weight loss at 80 weeks (12mg) in the pivotal Phase 3 TRIUMPH-1, up to 30.3% at 104 weeks in higher-BMI patients ([AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/retatrutide-achieves-up-to-30-3-average-weight-loss-in-phase-3-triumph-1-trial), 2026); NDA filing estimated Q4 2026–Q1 2027 ([BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/lillys-retatrutide-tripleG-phase3-obesity-data/820851/), 2026-05-21).
- Novo's amylin combo CagriSema hit ~23% weight loss over 84 weeks (REDEFINE 4) and was filed with FDA in Dec 2025 ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/drug-delivery/novo-slashes-glp-1-prices-again-touts-new-weight-loss-data-on-heels-of-lilly-loss), 2026).

**Exposed:** `LLY` (orforglipron, retatrutide), `NVO` (CagriSema, amycretin, oral Wegovy), `VKTX` (Viking — VK2735 oral/injectable), `GPCR` (Structure Therapeutics — oral small-molecule GLP-1), `ZEAL` (Zealand — amylin petrelintide, Roche-partnered), `RHHBY` (Roche — amylin via Carmot), `AMGN` (MariTide). Note: oral small molecules are a *headwind* to peptide CDMOs and device makers.

**Status rationale:** CURRENT — pills are the 2026 story and moving toward consensus fast; the still-pre-consensus wrinkle is which *mechanism* (amylin, glucagon) wins second-line, and the muscle problem it creates (below).

#### [FRONTIER] Muscle-preservation / body-composition drugs (the frontier bet)

**Claim:** 25–40% of GLP-1 weight loss is lean mass, so the next clinical necessity is an add-on that preserves or builds muscle — a category with no Phase 3 approval yet and therefore genuinely pre-consensus.

**Evidence:**
- Phase 2 bimagrumab + semaglutide produced body-composition improvements "previously unseen," with ~42% total fat-mass loss at 48 weeks at the top dose while sparing lean mass ([ConscienHealth / Nature Medicine](https://conscienhealth.org/2026/03/09/striking-results-with-bimagrumab-and-semaglutide/), 2026-03-09).
- As of mid-2025 *no* muscle-preserving agent had reached Phase 3, though several (incl. Lilly's bimagrumab) were poised to enter ([IQVIA](https://www.iqvia.com/locations/emea/blogs/2025/06/beyond-weight-loss), 2025-06).
- Signal of a still-unsettled field: Lilly *terminated* one bimagrumab combo trial in Sept 2025 for "strategic business reasons," while keeping others running ([BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/lilly-terminate-obesity-trial-bimagrumab-muscle-diabetes/761105/), 2025); the Lilly non-diabetic obesity bimagrumab+Zepbound trial (NCT06643728) had not posted results as of 2026-05-26 and none have surfaced since ([trial.medpath.com](https://trial.medpath.com/clinical-trial/6bce25081d503b55/nct06643728-bimagrumab-tirzepatide-obesity-overweight), updated 2026-05-26).
- Scholar Rock's own EMBRAZE Phase 2 (apitegromab + tirzepatide, obesity) reported **54.9% lean-mass preservation** vs. tirzepatide alone ([Scholar Rock investor release](https://investors.scholarrock.com/news-releases/news-release-details/scholar-rock-reports-positive-phase-2-embraze-trial-results), 2026-06-18) — real incremental de-risking, still Phase 2, no Phase 3 obesity plan announced.
- **Honesty flag:** SRRK's stock is up ~33% trailing 6 months (~4x SPY), but this is driven by an **unrelated program** — the apitegromab spinal-muscular-atrophy (SMA) BLA resubmission (PDUFA 2026-09-30) and a competitor's program drop, not by obesity/muscle-preservation news ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/scholar-rock-nasdaqsrrk-reaches-new-52-week-high-whats-next-2026-06-24/), 2026-06-24). Do not read SRRK's price action as the market pricing in this node — it is a false signal for this specific thesis.

**Exposed:** `LLY` (bimagrumab, via Versanis), `SRRK` (Scholar Rock — apitegromab/SRK-439 in obesity muscle; note its current run-up is SMA-driven, not obesity-driven, per above), `VERU` (Veru — enobosarm), `REGN` (Regeneron — trevogrumab/antibody combos with semaglutide), `BIOA` (BioAge). **Thin-evidence flag:** high binary risk, small-cap, no approved product — a watchlist/speculative node, not a de-risked thesis.

**Evidence update, falsifier trending toward triggering (2026-07-19):** as of this week, none of the three lead candidates has entered a Phase 3 obesity trial, and the lead candidate lost ground. Lilly terminated its Phase 2b bimagrumab+tirzepatide trial (obesity/overweight + T2D) on 2026-06-10 "for strategic business reasons," before enrollment completed — a separate Lilly Phase 2 trial in non-diabetic obese/overweight patients continues, results expected later in 2026 ([Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/eli-lilly-withdraws-phase-2b-trial-19b-obesity-bet-starting-enrollment), 2026-06-10; [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/lilly-terminate-obesity-trial-bimagrumab-muscle-diabetes/761105/), 2026-06-10). Regeneron's trevogrumab (COURAGE) remains Phase 2 (605 patients, interim readouts show 50–80% lean-mass preservation vs. semaglutide monotherapy), not due to complete until late 2026 ([Regeneron investor release](https://investor.regeneron.com/news-releases/news-release-details/results-phase-2-courage-trial-demonstrating-potential-improve/)). Scholar Rock's apitegromab (EMBRAZE) also remains Phase 2 with no Phase 3 obesity initiation announced; SRRK's only near-term Phase 3-adjacent catalyst is a BLA resubmission for apitegromab in the *unrelated* spinal-muscular-atrophy indication (FDA decision expected September 2026) ([Scholar Rock investor release](https://investors.scholarrock.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fda-issues-complete-response-letter-crl-apitegromab-treatment)). SRRK's Q2 2026 results are due 2026-08-06.

**Dies if:** no muscle-preservation candidate (bimagrumab, apitegromab, trevogrumab, or other) advances to a Phase 3 obesity trial by end of 2026, or a Phase 3 readout fails to show a clinically meaningful lean-mass-preservation benefit.

**Status rationale:** FRONTIER, held — clinically inevitable narrative, commercially unproven; the clearest "positioned before consensus" node in the tree, with correspondingly high failure risk. This week's evidence (Lilly's bimagrumab pullback, all three leads still Phase 2 with just over five months left in 2026) argues the falsifier is now closer to triggering than the prior status rationale implied — a real risk the node dies on a technicality (no Phase 3 start) even if none of the underlying science actually fails. Not a demotion yet; explicitly the top DIG DEEPER watch item.

### [CURRENT] Food & beverage demand shifts

**Claim:** GLP-1s cut calorie intake and reallocate food/beverage spend toward protein and away from snacks and alcohol — real at the category level, but the "apocalypse" framing overstates the aggregate hit.

**Evidence:**
- GLP-1 users consume ~21% fewer calories and cut grocery spend ~5.3% within six months of starting ([Food Business News / KPMG & Numerator](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/29532-glp-1-users-cut-food-spending-by-53), 2025); Cornell data shows savory-snack spend down ~10% among users ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2025/12/ozempic-changing-foods-americans-buy), 2025-12).
- **Counter-narrative (say-so):** aggregate USDA snack sales *held steady* through 2024–25 even as GLP-1 use rose — habit shifts, not collapse ([FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator-usa.com/Article/2026/03/05/snack-sales-stable-despite-glp-1-weight-loss-trends/), 2026-03-05).
- Spend is rotating *within* food: yogurt, fresh fruit, nutrition bars and meat snacks rose most among users ([EurekAlert / Cornell](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1110869), 2025).

**Exposed:** See grandchildren. Net: protein = winner, indulgent snacks/alcohol = pressured but resilient.

#### [CURRENT] Snacks & indulgent food (pressured, not collapsing)

**Claim:** Discretionary sweet/savory-snack makers face a slow demand drag, but diversified portfolios and habit-substitution blunt the impact.

**Evidence:** Savory snacks down ~10%, sweets/cookies similar among users ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2025/12/ozempic-changing-foods-americans-buy), 2025-12); yet USDA aggregate snack sales stable ([FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator-usa.com/Article/2026/03/05/snack-sales-stable-despite-glp-1-weight-loss-trends/), 2026-03-05); ~70% of users who eat less say they're snacking less (EY-Parthenon, via [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/21/glp-1-diets-restaurants-protein-fiber-weight-loss-drugs.html), 2026-03-21).

**Exposed (pressured):** `MDLZ` (Mondelez), `PEP` (PepsiCo/Frito-Lay), `HSY` (Hershey), `UTZ` (Utz), `JJSF` (J&J Snack Foods), `KHC` (Kraft Heinz). Most are reformulating toward protein/portion control rather than shrinking.

**Status rationale:** CURRENT — widely discussed, and the "overhyped" nuance is itself now semi-consensus; limited edge unless a specific pure-play snack name reprices.

#### [CURRENT] High-protein & GLP-1 companion nutrition (winner)

**Claim:** Appetite suppression forces users toward protein-dense, portion-controlled foods and supplements, creating a durable demand tailwind for protein brands and meal-replacement makers.

**Evidence:**
- Danone is investing ~$138M in a high-protein "Agile Nutrition" model; its high-protein segment (Oikos, HiPro, GetPRO) held double-digit growth through 2025 ([FoodIngredientsFirst](https://www.foodingredientsfirst.com/news/danone-2025-results-high-protein-growth.html), 2025).
- Dairy majors bought meal-replacement brands — Danone→Huel, Nestlé→Yfood, Lactalis→Protein Works — as GLP-1 reshapes nutrition ([DairyReporter](https://www.dairyreporter.com/Article/2026/06/11/dairy-majors-turn-to-meal-replacements-as-glp1-reshapes-nutrition/), 2026-06-11).
- The GLP-1 "companion products" market was ~$205B in 2025, projected ~$226B in 2026 ([Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/glp-1-companion-products-market-report), 2026).

**Exposed (winners):** `BRBR` (BellRing/Premier Protein — cleanest US pure-play), `SMPL` (Simply Good Foods/Quest, Atkins — **caveat added 2026-07-12**: Q3 FY26 net sales -6.3% YoY, swung to a $52.0M net loss, guidance cut to a 6-7% FY sales decline; CEO stated the miss is "largely execution driven rather than category driven" — concentrated in the Atkins brand's distribution losses and weak marketing, not a GLP-1-category reversal ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001702744/000170274426000021/a991_pressreleasexq3x2026.htm), 2026-07-09; [Yahoo Finance earnings-call highlights](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/simply-good-foods-co-smpl-210025744.html), 2026-07-09)), `DANOY` (Danone), `NSRGY` (Nestlé/Vital Pursuit), `LWAY` (Lifeway), `HLF` (Herbalife), `VITL` (Vital Farms — protein/eggs).

**Update (2026-07-19):** SMPL's Q3 FY2026 GlobeNewswire release confirms the numbers with primary-source precision — net sales $357.0M vs. $381.0M prior year ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/09/3324686/0/en/the-simply-good-foods-company-reports-fiscal-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-and-updates-fiscal-year-2026-outlook.html), 2026-07-09). BRBR named a new CEO, Michael Axelrod (ex-Snak King/Del Real Foods/Passport Food Group, prior TreeHouse/Kraft), effective 2026-07-29, succeeding Darcy Davenport — a governance fact, not a demand signal ([BellRing Brands](https://bellring.com/news/bellring-brands-announces-timing-third-quarter-fiscal-year-2026)). BRBR's Q3 FY2026 earnings are confirmed for **2026-08-04**, 7:00am ET — no pre-announcement or guidance update found ahead of that date.

**Status rationale:** CURRENT — the tailwind is real and BRBR/SMPL are recognized plays; edge is in second-derivative (private-label, ingredient suppliers) more than the obvious names. SMPL's Q3 miss does not change this: BRBR's most recent report (Q2, 2026-05-05) still showed +2% net sales growth, and BRBR's own Q3 doesn't land until 2026-08-04 — too early to read a company-specific Atkins stumble as a node-wide reversal. Revisit after BRBR reports.

#### [FRONTIER] Alcohol (real clinical signal, murky sales attribution)

**Claim:** GLP-1s dampen the reward pathway and measurably cut drinking, a structural long-term headwind for beverage-alcohol — but current sales weakness is hard to attribute cleanly.

**Evidence:**
- Meta-analysis: GLP-1 RAs significantly reduce alcohol intake, relapse and alcohol-related diagnoses ([eClinicalMedicine / Lancet](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/eclinm/article/PIIS2589-5370(25)00579-6/fulltext), 2025); Morgan Stanley modeled up to ~75% reduction in consumption among users ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_us/insights/consumer-products/glp-1-shifts-alcohol-market-dynamics), 2025).
- **Honesty flag:** attribution is contested — beer/wine/spirits declines coincide with affordability and secular sobriety trends, and IWSR data shows Gen Z past-6-month drinking actually *rose* from 66% (Mar 2023) to 73% (Mar 2025) ([FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/02/13/alcohol-decline-drivers-affordability-glp-1-drugs-health/), 2026-02-13).
- Among users who cut back, wine falls most (~52%), then beer (~43%), spirits (~40%) ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_us/insights/consumer-products/glp-1-shifts-alcohol-market-dynamics), 2025).

**Exposed (pressured):** `STZ` (Constellation), `DEO` (Diageo), `BUD` (AB InBev), `TAP` (Molson Coors), `SAM` (Boston Beer), `BF.B` (Brown-Forman). Wine/spirits-heavy names most exposed.

**Status rationale:** FRONTIER/SPECULATIVE — clinically credible, commercially unproven as an attributable sales driver; the alcohol bear thesis is a multi-year option, not a 2026 catalyst.

### [CURRENT] Healthcare procedure & device second-orders (winners & losers)

**Claim:** Pharmacological weight loss substitutes for or delays several surgical/device interventions (bariatric surgery, sleep-apnea implants, some dialysis) while *expanding* eligibility for others (joint replacement) — a clean winners-and-losers split.

**Evidence:** See grandchildren. The reflexive "GLP-1 kills all medtech" trade is wrong in at least two nodes (ResMed, ortho).

#### [CONSENSUS] Bariatric surgery (loser)

**Claim:** GLP-1s are cannibalizing elective bariatric surgery volumes.

**Evidence:** GLP-1 scripts grew from <4,600 (2018) to >1.4M (2025) while bariatric-surgery utilization fell from ~0.24% (2023) to ~0.21% (2024–25), the sharpest decline since modern GLP-1s launched ([ASMBS](https://asmbs.org/news_releases/as-glp-1-use-skyrockets-and-bariatric-surgery-slows-most-obesity-goes-untreated/), 2025).

**Exposed (losers):** `MDT` (Medtronic — bariatric/stapling), `ISRG` (Intuitive — indirect, bariatric a small mix), hospital systems with bariatric programs. No large pure-play; impact is diffuse.

**Status rationale:** CONSENSUS — well-established and already reflected; limited edge.

#### [CURRENT] Sleep-apnea devices (loser, contrarian nuance)

**Claim:** Zepbound's OSA approval pressures implant/CPAP demand — but GLP-1s also drive *diagnosis*, splitting implant makers (hurt) from CPAP (net beneficiary).

**Evidence:**
- Tirzepatide cut apnea-hypopnea index by ~25–29 events/hr vs ~5 for placebo, and is FDA-approved for OSA — a direct therapeutic substitute ([PMC systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13109165/), 2025).
- Inspire Medical stock crashed ~37% on GLP-1 pressure plus an Inspire V delay; Q1 2026 revenue growth slowed to ~1.6% ([AInvest](https://www.ainvest.com/news/inspire-medical-insp-plummets-40-perfect-storm-guidance-cuts-glp-1-competition-2508/), 2025).
- **Contrarian nuance:** ~half of even surgically-slimmed patients still need CPAP, and analysts argue GLP-1s *raise OSA awareness/diagnosis* — supportive for CPAP volumes ([AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/glp-1s-likely-new-frontier-in-the-treatment-of-osa-obesity-in-sleep-medicine), 2025).

**Exposed:** `INSP` (Inspire — clear loser, implant substitution), `RMD` (ResMed — the counter-trade: GLP-1 as diagnosis funnel, arguably a winner), Gerresheimer / other CPAP components.

**Status rationale:** CURRENT — Inspire's de-rating is consensus; the still-debated edge is whether ResMed is a *beneficiary*, which the bearish knee-jerk misprices.

#### [CURRENT] Orthopedics / joint replacement (contrarian winner)

**Claim:** Contrary to the "GLP-1 shrinks all surgery" reflex, weight loss makes obese patients *eligible* for knee/hip replacement and lowers surgical complications — net neutral-to-positive for implant makers.

**Evidence:**
- Stryker's knee GM: "We don't anticipate the weight-loss drug having a negative impact. Actually, we think it's going to help with implant volumes" ([Medical Design & Outsourcing](https://www.medicaldesignandoutsourcing.com/stryker-glp-1-weight-loss-drugs-knee-hip-joint-implant-replacement-volume/), 2024).
- GLP-1 use among total-knee-arthroplasty patients rose from 4% (2019) to 16% (2023); pre-op GLP-1 use is associated with lower complication rates ([Becker's](https://www.beckersspine.com/orthopedic/61564-experts-cast-wary-eye-on-glp-1s-effects-in-orthopedics.html), 2025).
- Retatrutide's TRIUMPH-4 showed substantial osteoarthritis *pain relief* alongside weight loss — a cross-node link that could *delay* some replacements even as it expands the eligible pool ([Lilly](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-weight-loss-average), 2026).

**Exposed (net winners/neutral):** `SYK` (Stryker), `ZBH` (Zimmer Biomet). Two-sided risk: OA pain relief could push out procedures at the margin.

**Status rationale:** CURRENT — the market oscillates between "loser" and "winner"; the eligibility-expansion case is under-weighted relative to the substitution fear.

#### [FRONTIER] Dialysis / renal (loser, long-duration)

**Claim:** Semaglutide slows CKD progression, threatening the long-run dialysis-patient funnel — a slow-burn structural headwind for dialysis providers.

**Evidence:**
- FLOW trial: semaglutide cut major kidney events 24%, was stopped a year early for efficacy, and drove FDA approval (Jan 2025) for reducing eGFR decline/ESKD in T2D+CKD ([HCPLive](https://www.hcplive.com/view/flow-trial-fortifies-semaglutide-s-role-in-chronic-kidney-disease-and-type-2-diabetes), 2025).
- DaVita and Fresenius shares fell when FLOW was halted early as investors priced a smaller future dialysis population ([diaTribe](https://diatribe.org/diabetes-medications/ozempic-slows-kidney-disease-now-fda-approved-ckd), 2025).

**Exposed (losers):** `DVA` (DaVita), `FMS` (Fresenius Medical Care), `OM` (Outset Medical), `BAX` (Baxter — renal exposure). **Thin-evidence flag on timing:** kidney-disease progression is multi-decade; near-term dialysis volumes are largely insulated, so this is a long-duration option, not a 2026 P&L event.

**Status rationale:** FRONTIER — directionally clear, but the payoff horizon is so long the market can keep it un-priced; genuinely pre-consensus on *magnitude*.

### [CURRENT] Insurance / PBM / employer & government coverage

**Claim:** Who *pays* for GLP-1s — employers, PBMs, Medicare — is the swing variable for volume, and 2026 policy is materially expanding covered access.

**Evidence:**
- The Medicare GLP-1 "Bridge" launched July 1, 2026, offering Wegovy, Zepbound KwikPen and Foundayo at $50/month for eligible Part D members through end-2027 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/30/medicare-obesity-drug-glp-1-coverage-starting-july-1.html), 2026-06-30).
- Medicare already saw ~21.8M GLP-1 claims and ~$27.5B gross spend in 2024 (across indications) — the fiscal stakes forcing PBM/insurer cost management ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicare/what-to-know-about-the-balance-model-for-glp-1s-in-medicare-and-medicaid/), 2026).
- Novo cut Wegovy's list price ~50% (to ~$675/month, effective Jan 1 2027) to ease access barriers and match Lilly on price ([pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/novo-nordisk-cut-glp-1-prices-tough-us-market), 2026).

**Exposed:** `UNH` (UnitedHealth), `CVS` (Caremark PBM + retail dispensing), `CI` (Cigna/Express Scripts), `HIMS` (Hims & Hers — now a Novo-authorized telehealth channel after the March 2026 settlement; a cash-pay access winner), `GDRX` (GoodRx — cash-pay discount rails). Two-sided: coverage expansion lifts volume (good for dispensers) but raises payer cost (margin pressure for insurers).

**Update — payer channels are bifurcating (2026-07-19):** CVS Caremark will reinstate Zepbound as a preferred formulary option effective **2026-10-01**, alongside Lilly's oral Foundayo — reversing its July 2025 decision to drop Zepbound, potentially restoring access for 25–30M lives ([CVS Health](https://www.cvshealth.com/news/company-news/cvs-caremark-delivers-affordability-and-access-to-glp-1-weight-management-medications-with-expanded-coverage-options.html), 2026-05-28). Against that, a Mercer survey found 6% of large employers (500+) dropped GLP-1 coverage in 2026 and another 5% are considering dropping it for 2027 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/26/glp1-weight-loss-drug-pills-insurance-plan-coverage.html), 2026-06-26), and only 13 states now cover GLP-1s for weight loss under Medicaid, down from 16 after CA/NH/PA/SC eliminated the obesity indication. Net read: PBM/federal payers (CVS Caremark, CMS's Medicare Bridge) are expanding access while employer/Medicaid payers pull back on cost grounds — a genuinely two-sided, unresolved coverage picture, not a one-directional tailwind.

**Status rationale:** CURRENT — the July 1 Medicare bridge and price cuts are fresh, actively repricing HIMS/CVS/insurer names; among the most catalyst-rich near-term nodes. This week's bifurcated employer-vs-PBM coverage evidence argues against ever calling this CONSENSUS in one direction — the "who pays" fight is still live on both sides.
