## NEW CANDIDATES

Six candidates, each stated as a chain from consensus root to a frontier node the market is under-weighting. All are logged to the scoreboard at today's close.

### 1. West Pharmaceutical (`WST`) — GLP-1 · $365.74
**Chain:** GLP-1 mass adoption (CONSENSUS) → every injectable dose needs a coated stopper + prefilled component (injectable supply chain) → **qualified fill-finish capacity is the physical chokepoint that survives even as drug list prices fall** (CURRENT, contrarian).
**Why it's a candidate:** West makes the elastomer stoppers and components that every injectable GLP-1 pen requires, in a concentrated oligopoly where *qualified* (validated, regulator-approved) capacity — not raw material — is the binding constraint ([Pharma Bottlenecks](https://pharmabottlenecks.com/chokepoints/drug-packaging-components), 2025). The stock has been de-rated on the "oral pills kill injectables" fear, which the installed injectable base and multi-year dosing make look overdone.
**What would kill the thesis:** faster-than-expected shift to oral small molecules (orforglipron bypasses injection entirely) and to monthly/quarterly dosing, both of which cut devices-per-patient. This is the real bear case, not a strawman.
**Theme tags:** `glp1-supply-chain`, `healthcare-services`.

### 2. Scholar Rock (`SRRK`) — GLP-1 · $54.88
**Chain:** GLP-1 weight loss (CONSENSUS) → 25–40% of the weight lost is *lean muscle mass* → **a muscle-preserving add-on becomes the next clinical necessity** (FRONTIER — no Phase 3 approval exists yet, genuinely pre-consensus).
**Why it's a candidate:** the muscle-loss problem is clinically inevitable and there is no approved fix; Phase 2 bimagrumab + semaglutide data was described as "previously unseen" ([ConscienHealth/Nature Medicine](https://conscienhealth.org/2026/03/09/striking-results-with-bimagrumab-and-semaglutide/), 2026-03-09). Scholar Rock (apitegromab / SRK-439) is a small-cap pure-play on exactly this.
**What would kill the thesis — read this before anything else:** this is a **high binary-risk, small-cap biotech with no approved product.** A single failed readout can halve it. It is on the scoreboard as a FRONTIER bet precisely so we track whether these speculative calls actually pay — not because it's de-risked. Size accordingly, or treat purely as a watch item.
**Theme tags:** `healthcare-services`.

### 3. ResMed (`RMD`) — GLP-1 · $209.63
**Chain:** GLP-1s now treat obstructive sleep apnea (Zepbound OSA approval) → reflexive market trade: "GLP-1 kills sleep-apnea hardware" → **but GLP-1s also drive OSA *diagnosis*, and ~half of even slimmed patients still need CPAP** → CPAP is a net beneficiary, not a victim (CURRENT, contrarian winner).
**Why it's a candidate:** the knee-jerk lumps ResMed (CPAP) with Inspire (surgical implant, a genuine substitution loser, down ~37%). But analysts argue GLP-1s expand the diagnosed OSA funnel, and the clinical data shows most patients still need airway support ([AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/glp-1s-likely-new-frontier-in-the-treatment-of-osa-obesity-in-sleep-medicine), 2025). The mispricing is the market treating a possible beneficiary as a loser.
**What would kill the thesis:** if higher-efficacy molecules (retatrutide-class, ~30% weight loss) resolve enough OSA to genuinely shrink the CPAP pool, the "diagnosis funnel" tailwind loses to the substitution headwind.
**Theme tags:** `healthcare-services`.

### 4. Fabrinet (`FN`) — AI infrastructure · $499.61
**Chain:** AI clusters scale to millions of GPUs (CONSENSUS) → the bottleneck shifts from FLOPs to bandwidth-per-watt → optics is the fastest-growing bill-of-materials line (CURRENT; Nvidia paid $4B for Coherent/Lumentum stakes) → **co-packaged optics shifts value toward the laser/fiber-attach assembly that CPO can't integrate away** (FRONTIER).
**Why it's a candidate:** 1.6T optical module demand is projected to grow from ~1.8M units (2025) to 30M+ (2026) ([MapYourTech](https://mapyourtech.com/co-packaged-optics-architecture-status-and-the-path-to-1-6t-switches/), 2026). Fabrinet is the contract manufacturer assembling much of Nvidia's optics — a way to hold the optics ramp without picking which transceiver design wins.
**What would kill the thesis:** heavy customer concentration (Nvidia + a few others); a faster-than-expected move to fully integrated CPO could disintermediate discrete-module assembly. The optics primes (COHR/LITE) already re-rated hard, so easy discovery here is gone — FN is the less-crowded expression, not an undiscovered one.
**Theme tags:** `ai-infrastructure`, `data-center-hardware`.

### 5. Cleveland-Cliffs (`CLF`) — AI infrastructure / grid (cross-node) · $9.86
**Chain:** AI + electrification → transformer shortage (4–5 year lead times) → **every transformer core needs grain-oriented electrical steel, and the US has exactly one domestic producer** (FRONTIER, single-source chokepoint).
**Why it's a candidate:** Cliffs' Butler Works is the sole US GOES producer, and the Pentagon just locked in a GOES supply contract worth up to $400M through 2030 ([Defence Blog](https://defence-blog.com/pentagon-locks-in-critical-transformer-steel-supply/), 2026) — a chokepoint the market isn't pricing because CLF trades as a distressed *carbon-steel* cyclical, not a GOES story. This is the same node in two trees (AI-infra materials and grid transformers), which is exactly the cross-sector convergence the machine is built to catch.
**What would kill the thesis — flagged honestly:** GOES is a *small slice* of CLF's consolidated P&L, which is dominated by cyclical carbon steel and a levered balance sheet. The GOES thesis can be right and the stock still be driven by steel prices and debt. Evidence on GOES's actual earnings contribution is thin — this is a chokepoint idea wearing a commodity-cyclical costume. Size for that.
**Theme tags:** `materials`, `grid-equipment`.

### 6. Generac (`GNRC`) — grid modernization · $252.66
**Chain:** Structural electricity demand + 4–7 year interconnection queues (CONSENSUS demand, real scarcity) → large loads buy their own on-site power → **behind-the-meter generation, where Generac's data-center backlog is inflecting off a home-standby base** (CURRENT).
**Why it's a candidate:** Generac's data-center backlog grew from $400M to ~$700M in 2025–26, built from colocators before any hyperscaler mega-award ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/generacs-700-million-data-center-backlog-is-building-the-case-for-a-250-price-target), 2026). The July PJM emergency — DOE ordering large users onto backup generation — validated the "bridge power becomes grid infrastructure" thesis in real time. Less crowded than CAT/Cummins because the market still frames Generac as a residential-generator story.
**What would kill the thesis:** the data-center backlog is still small relative to the home-standby core, which is weather- and rate-sensitive; if hyperscaler awards route to CAT/Cummins/Bloom instead, the inflection stalls.
**Theme tags:** `power-generation`, `grid-equipment`.
